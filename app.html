    User journey

    From first contact to a lifelong financial companion.

    The three-stage logic comes from the base prototype (Pigtie, presented at TUM): first the person feels heard, then gets concrete guidance, and only then does the app become a weekly habit. Each stage is adapted to the frictions and account-opening triggers found in the group's survey of young Brazilians.

    1
    Day 1 · First contact

    100% digital onboarding and a first conversation

    Profile-based onboarding (student, first job, own business, under 18 with a guardian). No branch visit, no fees on the basic package, plain language. The AI starts by asking, not by selling.

    "Honestly, I don't know where to start."

    Rationale & evidence

    Public-bank trust with a digital-bank experience.

    Group value proposition: for young Brazilians aged 18 to 25 who need a trusted, simple and personalized banking experience, the Youth Financial Companion is an AI-powered app journey that helps them manage money, start investing and access relevant products with less friction.

    What the group's survey showed

    57.5%

    of 13 to 18 year-olds use only a digital bank (n=40).

    72.3%

    of cited frictions concentrate on physical branches, fees and bureaucracy (n=83).

    0.39

    point trust gap between traditional and digital banks among 13 to 18 year-olds, the smallest in the sample (1 to 5 scale).

    75%

    of 13 to 18 year-olds say their parents chose or recommended their current bank.

    Source: "The Future of Finance" survey run by the group (N=83; 40 aged 13 to 18). Convenience sample: percentages guide hypotheses, not population-level inference.

    Note for the team: age rangeThe value proposition and pilot target 18 to 25, but the survey focused on 13 to 18 and showed the first bank is largely a parents' decision. The prototype handles this with a "Family" module (dependent account opened by the guardian) as an entry point, while the main pilot stays at 18 to 25. The team should decide explicitly whether this module goes into the pilot or a second phase.

    Why the credit card is in scope

    A real trigger

    13.3% of respondents opened their first account because they "wanted my own card to buy something". The card is a tangible reason to choose a bank, not just a feature.

    Personalization people can see

    The case deck lists personalized experience and smart product recommendations as core gains. Designing your own digital card is the most visible, low-cost form of personalization.

    Responsible credit by design

    A starter limit, a personal spending cap below the approved limit, auto-pay in full by default and AI nudges on utilization. Limits and approval depend on the bank's credit policy, to be defined with Risk.

    How each principle becomes a screen

    Proactive and personalized

    The AI reacts to events (salary landed, spending above limit, bill due soon) and suggests the next best action in one tap.

    • AI chat
    • Home insights
    • Card studio
    • Monthly review

    Frictionless and conversational

    Young users ask in natural language instead of hunting through menus. Account and card in minutes, no branch, no fees on basic services.

    • Welcome and Profile
    • Spending limits
    • Instant digital card

    Trustworthy and transparent

    Every recommendation shows "why am I seeing this", the data used and a way to decline. Trust is the asset the incumbent still has.

    • Card controls (freeze, caps)
    • Privacy and data

    Integration options to discuss

    Recommended for pilot

    Embedded in the current app

    Youth journey inside the existing app, reusing login, products and customer base. Lower acquisition cost, higher dependency on the IT roadmap.

    Alternative

    Separate youth app or brand

    More freedom in language and design. Requires its own acquisition and may dilute brand trust, which is the differentiator.

    Complementary

    Conversational channel (WhatsApp)

    The AI also talks where young people already are, with sensitive actions completed in the app. Good for nudges and reminders.

    Validation plan

    First prove young people want it and use it, then build.

    The main blocker identified by the group is the lack of validated evidence that young customers actually want and would use the companion. This prototype exists to unblock that: usability tests with 5 to 8 young people per round before any investment in development.

    Usability test script (30 to 40 min per person)

    1

    Open the account

    "You just got an internship. Open your account and tell me what you think." Watch for hesitation, unclear terms and time to complete.

    2

    Ask the AI for help

    "Your salary just landed and you want to save for a trip. Use the app." Check whether they find the chat on their own and accept the suggestion.

    3

    Control spending

    "You think you're spending too much on delivery. What would you do?" Check whether they find Limits and understand the alert.

    4

    Design your card

    "Make this card yours." Observe which options they use, how long they spend and whether they set a spending cap or turn off international purchases.

    5

    Understand the bill

    "Your card bill is due soon. What does the app recommend and why?" Ask them to explain interest and paying in full in their own words.

    6

    First investment

    "You have R$ 100 left over each month. What does the app suggest?" Ask them to explain liquidity and risk in their own words.

    7

    Trust an offer

    "Why is the app showing you this offer?" Test whether the explanation increases or reduces trust.

    8

    Wrap-up

    Would you use it weekly? Would you switch from your current app? How likely are you to recommend it (0 to 10)? What would you remove?
